# Philosophy

Local Protocol is an open coordination protocol for local commerce. It lets
independent delivery operators interoperate without merging, centralizing, or
handing control to a single platform.

## The fundamental belief

Local commerce already works. What breaks is coordination.

Most delivery platforms solve coordination by centralizing power: one database,
one brand, one fee structure, one set of rules. It’s efficient, but it erodes
local autonomy, margins, resilience, and innovation.

Local Protocol takes a different stance: coordination should be shared, not
owned.

## What Local Protocol is (conceptually)

Local Protocol is not:

- A delivery app
- A marketplace
- A company that owns customers, drivers, or merchants
- A replacement for existing delivery businesses

Local Protocol is:

- A shared language for local commerce
- A neutral coordination layer that multiple delivery companies can plug into
- A way for independent operators to interoperate as peers, not subsidiaries

Think of it like:

- Email (SMTP) for delivery orders
- Payment rails for fulfillment and settlement
- DNS for discovering local services

## The core abstraction

Local Protocol models food delivery as verifiable commitments between
independent parties:

- A merchant commits to preparing food
- A courier commits to fulfilling delivery
- A consumer commits to payment
- A delivery company commits to coordination and support

Instead of one company owning the entire transaction, each party keeps its
autonomy while the protocol records what was promised, by whom, and under what
conditions. This lets multiple delivery companies—even competitors—coordinate
safely within the same local market.

## Why interoperability matters

Today, delivery companies compete by:

- Locking in merchants
- Exclusivity contracts
- Walled-off courier networks
- Fragmented consumer demand

Local Protocol enables a different dynamic:

- Companies compete on service quality, logistics, pricing, and experience
- While sharing a common substrate for orders, fulfillment, and settlement

This unlocks:

- Cross-network delivery
- Shared courier liquidity
- Local-first networks that scale horizontally
- Lower fees without sacrificing reliability

## Power distribution philosophy

Local Protocol is designed so that:

- No single operator controls the network
- Exit is always possible
- Forking is always possible
- Local operators retain identity and brand

If a delivery company disappears, the local network continues. If a city wants
to run its own instance, it can. If a group of merchants wants different
economics, they can coordinate without permission.

## Economic philosophy

Most delivery platforms extract value by:

- Taxing transactions
- Owning demand
- Enforcing opaque algorithms

Local Protocol shifts this model:

- Value accrues to participants, not the protocol
- The protocol enables coordination but does not dictate economics
- Fees, incentives, and subsidies are local decisions

This enables city-backed incentives, cooperative ownership models,
merchant-aligned pricing, and experimentation without platform approval.

## Long-term vision

Local Protocol imagines a future where:

- Food delivery is infrastructure, not a monopoly
- Local markets are resilient to single-company failure
- Innovation happens at the edges, not the center
- Delivery companies become network participants, not gatekeepers

In this world, a delivery company doesn’t have to win the market to succeed. It
just has to serve it well.
